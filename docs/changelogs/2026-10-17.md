### Reconnect Roomba

Christian asked for a "hardware reset" button for when the Roomba dies. Until
now, after the Roomba was turned off and on, `roomba_bridge` had to be restarted
(Stop + Start mapping), which also threw away the map being built.

- `roomba_bridge` (`lucia_control`): new service `~/reconnect`
  (`/roomba_bridge/reconnect`, `std_srvs/Trigger`). It stops the wheels, reopens
  the serial port, wakes the Roomba (BRC pulse; only does something if the cable
  has that wire), sends Start (128) and Safe (131), and plays a short beep: since
  nothing comes back from the Roomba over serial, the beep is the confirmation.
  Takes about 5 s. Serial errors while driving (adapter unplugged) are now logged
  once instead of crashing the node; a failed reconnect leaves it running with
  the wheels stopped.
- `RoombaOI.beep()` (song opcodes 140/141, slot 3), ROS2 copy only, with a byte
  test; `lucia_control` has 22 tests.
- `web_ui`: `/api/roomba/reconnect` stops a running mission first (so it doesn't
  drive off the moment the Roomba wakes), calls the service and returns its
  answer. The page has a **Reconnect Roomba** button in the Mapping card.
- Checked here: the reconnect, error and shutdown paths with ROS and the Roomba
  faked out; the button against the stub (43/43 browser checks). Not yet tried

### Later: gradual speed, stuck check, pause and carry on

Christian, after `main` was updated: the Roomba's brushes and vacuum are removed,
so it's only a drive base for mapping (no cleaning features). Explore stutters a
bit over uneven ground; speed changes should be gradual to keep the hardware on
board safe. Wanted: stuck detection, and driving by hand should only pause
Explore, which carries on after a couple of seconds.

**Gradual speed** (`lucia_control`): `roomba_bridge` no longer sends the newest
`/cmd_vel` straight to the wheels. Each 0.1 s tick moves partway toward it
(`motion.ramp_toward`, tested): speeding up 0.3 m/s² (0 to 15 cm/s in 0.5 s),
slowing down 0.6 m/s² (15 cm/s to a stop in 0.3 s, about 2 cm), turning 1.5 /
3.0 rad/s². It covers every driver (Nav2, the mission node, the page) and every
stop (STOP, quiet `/cmd_vel`, the ToF gate); only Reconnect and shutdown stop at
once. Commands are first capped to what the wheels can do (a 5 m/s command would
otherwise leave the ramp seconds away from stopping). `/odom` is integrated from
the ramped speeds. Checked here by running the real drive tick against a fake
Roomba.

**The stutter is not explained yet.** Two likely causes, and the test that tells
them apart:
- The Roomba stopping itself. In Safe mode a cliff sensor or wheel-drop switch
  (a bump, a rug edge) drops it to Passive and the motors stop; the 1 s Safe-mode
  heartbeat restarts it. Nothing tells the Pi (no serial replies), so the ramp
  can't smooth that restart.
- Nav2's commands jumping: the lidar tilting on uneven ground adds floor hits to
  the costmap (cost-scaled slowdowns), or slam_toolbox corrects the open-loop
  position and the path shifts. The ramp smooths these.
- Test: hold W over the same patch, by hand, at the same speed. If it still
  stutters, it's the Roomba (Safe mode). The page's "Speed (wheels)" metric shows
  what the wheels are told: steady while the robot stutters = the Roomba side.

**Stuck check** (`lucia_nav/motion_check.py`, tested): the wheels can't report
(open-loop `/odom`), and Nav2's progress checker watches that same odom, so
nothing noticed a robot that's stuck. Now, twice a second, the mission node
compares what `/odom` says the wheels were told with what really happened:
- turning: the IMU heading change against the commanded turn;
- driving: the newest scan against one from about 1 s (at least 12 cm of
  commanded travel) earlier, shifted by 0, 25, ... 125% of the commanded
  distance; the best fit is how far it really drove. Reference scans are joined
  into outlines (so a scan doesn't fit itself best just because beams land on
  the same spots), and walls seen edge-on are left out (same reason: in a bare
  hallway the first version leaned toward "didn't move" while driving).
  When every shift fits about as well, the answer is "can't tell", never stuck.
- 3 "not moving" answers in a row while driven = stuck (about 2 s after it stops
  moving, at 15 cm/s).
- Checked in a simulated room and hallway: 0 wrong out of 900 random spots and
  headings at 1-2 cm lidar noise; a bare hallway never reads "didn't move" while
  driving, even at 3 cm noise; a person walking by doesn't fool it; about 6 ms
  per check on the laptop, pure Python.

Recovery (`mission_logic.py`, tested): stop, play an "uh-oh", back up 15 cm at
8 cm/s if the lidar sees nothing in the strip behind, then Explore skips that
frontier and goes on, HOME / go-to try again. 3 times in a row with no goal
reached in between: stop and play a falling tune three times. Driving by hand it
only says so. The page shows **Really moving?** in Motion and a red **!** on the
map where it got stuck. `mission.py`'s new callbacks were run here with ROS
faked: a stuck robot was caught after 2.0 s and backed up; a moving one (forward,
an arc, turning in place, lidar mounted backwards like ours) was never flagged,
and with the lidar's mounting deliberately wrong it was, so the TF lookup matters
and is used.

**Pause and carry on**: `pause` / `resume` mission commands. Driving by hand
while it drives itself sends `pause` (was `stop`): it lets go of the wheels and
remembers what it was doing. `web_ui` sends `resume` 3 s after the last key
press (`resume_after_s`; 0 = never). Explore carries on with what it learned
(failed frontiers, stuck spots); HOME and go-to head for the same spot. STOP or
any other mission command cancels it, and the page counts down ("Back to
exploring in 3 s (STOP cancels)"). Letting go of a key while paused now stops
the wheels at once (it used to wait for the 0.3 s timeout). Checked against the
stub in a browser: pause, countdown, resume after about 4 s (3 s plus the page's
1 s poll), STOP cancels, stuck message and marks (14/14).

**Top speed, slower in tight spaces** (asked right after): Nav2 already slows a
little, but only with its center within 0.4 m of something, and it cruised at
15 cm/s (the slider's starting value). Now the slider and Nav2 start at the top
speed, 30 cm/s, and `safety_gate` caps forward speed from each lidar scan:
the room around the robot's front half (from its edge) and the free run ahead
in its own lane give a cap between 0.30 m/s (0.35 m of room, 0.8 m ahead) and
0.06 m/s (5 cm / 0.2 m). Turning slows in proportion, so the curve is kept;
backing up and turning in place pass; no fresh scan = 0.06. Run with fake ROS:
30 cm/s mid-room, 20 nearing a 0.7 m doorway, 10 in it, back to 30 past it,
17 heading at a wall 0.5 m away.
